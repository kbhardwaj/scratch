# Capstone: the Cooperation brief, the defense, the first 90 days

Status: written against `SPEC.md`, `course.json`, `synthesis/learning-design.md` (module ids m0..m11,
brief sections s1..s14 from seed 4c, casebook one-liners k01..k24 from seed 4d, capstone seed 4f),
`synthesis/claims-ledger.md` (section 5 binding; section 4 for every number), `synthesis/tensions.md`
(t01..t14, headings as of writing) and `synthesis/unified-model.md` (the twelve laws and the
five-station loop). `synthesis/casebook.md` and `synthesis/design-brief-template.md` did not exist
when this was written; the ids used here are the seeds those files must keep. The reconciliation log
at the end records every departure.

The capstone asks one question: **could you diagnose why a real group cooperates, coordinates or
fails to, design the norms, incentives, network structure and institutions that fix it, defend that
design under attack, and run its first ninety days as a learning system?** The brief is the design;
the defense is the design under pressure; the ninety-day plan is the design turned into a loop that
can tell you it was wrong.

## Deliverable 1: Cooperation brief v6

Use the skeleton fixed in `learning-design.md` seed 4c (s1-s14); `design-brief-template.md` is
authoritative once written and keeps these ids. The brief is about one real group the learner can
observe from the inside. Every declarative design sentence carries one tag. The minimum contents
below say what must be present; **a missing item caps the matching rubric criterion (in brackets)
at 2.**

- **s1 The group and its boundary.** Who is in and out, as a membership rule not a headcount; what
  is shared and whether it is subtractable; the reference network (the people whose behaviour
  members actually watch, not the org chart); the exclusion the boundary creates, tagged [V]. [1, 3]
- **s2 Diagnosis: the games being played.** For each target behaviour: players, options, ordinal
  payoffs, order and information, drawn as a matrix or tree; the written answer to "does a member
  gain by defecting when everyone else cooperates?"; the shape (dilemma, stag hunt, chicken,
  convention, or "not a game: an accounting constraint"); the fix set the shape licenses and the fix
  set it forbids; the classification tagged [H]. [1]
- **s3 The collective brain.** Who learns what from whom; which practices spread by modelling and
  which need teaching (ledger rule 8); single points of failure; which copying bias the group runs
  on (success, prestige, conformity), because s10 depends on it. [7]
- **s4 Situational audit and folk-psychology check.** Dispositional explanations from v0 re-read
  situationally; levers struck through because they sit on the failed-replication list (rule 20).
  A struck-through lever used elsewhere caps criterion 8 at 1. [8]
- **s5 Threshold and tipping map.** Whether behaviour is contingent on how many others do it; the
  rough threshold distribution; the minimum viable coalition k; any arithmetic constraint. [2]
- **s6 Population of types and fairness baseline.** The estimated type mix as [H] with the ledger's
  "rough" caveat; the fairness baseline; whether any process is weak-link and the size of the unit
  whose minimum matters. [2]
- **s7 Equilibrium-selection plan.** The regime (mixing, learning rule, talk, size, history); the
  correlation devices in cost order (lower the lone-cooperator cost, leader names the target, public
  opt-in pledges, gradual growth from a core with visible history, small stable units); the pacing
  rule; the lone-cooperator cost before and after; the switch signal toward risk dominance. [2]
- **s8 Norm register.** Every rule classified with Bicchieri's four-way taxonomy; for each social
  norm the empirical and the normative expectation as separate sentences; the four-question
  elicitation instrument with sample and date, plus the private-attitude question; cues;
  trendsetters chosen by position and sensitivity, not charisma. [3, 4]
- **s9 Institutional design.** The eight principles answered as questions, including "not binding
  here"; the sanction ladder with a small, quick, public first step; monitoring as a by-product of
  the work; the dispute forum; **standing: who may change which rules, in what arena, recognized by
  whom**; the external backstop; the boundary's cost as [V]. [5, 6]
- **s10 Network map and seeding plan.** Clusters and bridges of the reference network; which kind of
  copying the behaviour is (informational cascade, network effect, coordination on a graph, rule
  19); q = b/(a+b) estimated in words; the seed set inside the resisting cluster; bridges to widen;
  whether s3's learning rule lets clusters help. [7]
- **s11 The signal and the BPC statement.** The public cue that tells each member which equilibrium
  is in play; the two-part norm test (epistemic and motivational); what the design assumes about
  members' beliefs about each other, preferences and constraints; which of the four layers each
  element addresses. [10]
- **s12 Open hypotheses register.** Every [H] with its test, measure and date; at least eight. [8]
- **s13 Value commitments register.** Every [V] with the reading-list dissenter and the cost the
  learner accepts; at least six. [8]
- **s14 Measurement plan and ninety-day predictions.** Per lever: prediction with a number,
  practical measure and source, balancing measure, return time, abandon rule. [9]
- **Appendix: v0 changelog.** Line-by-line re-tag of the m0 page against v6: every reversal with
  the module and author that caused it; every v0 position kept, with reason; the rewritten free
  response beside the day-1 version; the retake with the calibration delta. Fewer than eight
  substantive reversals or no kept position caps criterion 8 at 3. [8]

s2, s7, s8, s9 and s10 carry the design and should take about two-thirds of the words; s3-s6 are
diagnostic and tight; s12-s14 are tables.

## Deliverable 2: The defense

**Part A** answers the `course.json` critique prompt against the learner's own v6 in 400-600 words,
written as the critic: (1) every tag arguably wrong, in both directions; (2) every contradiction with
the six named course teachings (game before fix; both expectation types; Ostrom's principles;
network structure shapes diffusion; social preferences real but conditional; risk dominance beats
payoff dominance under uncertainty); (3) inconsistencies between sections; (4) the single most
important missing decision. A Part A that finds nothing scores 1 on criterion 10: m11's hinge Q3
says the standing decision and the signal are missing from most briefs.

**Part B** answers six sealed challenges: four casebook cases transplanted into the learner's group
and two value attacks on the learner's own [V] commitments. Each answer is 200-300 words, or the six
are a 20-minute talk. Every answer makes five moves, in order:

1. **Name the tension** by id and name, and say which pole the challenge pushes toward.
2. **Apply the rule to the case and to the brief**, citing the section: which IF branch of the
   tension's rule the group is in, and what the cited section already says.
3. **State the switch signal**: the number or observation that would show the wrong pole was chosen,
   made specific to the group ("effort falls round on round in a unit above about a dozen" becomes
   "review turnaround in the twelve-person guild lengthens three weeks running").
4. **Say what you do not know, tagged**: which claim is [E] for the mechanism and [H] for this
   group; which is [V].
5. **Say what changes in the brief, or why nothing does.** At least one of the six answers must
   change the brief; the change is logged as v6.1 with an entry in s12 or s13.

Value challenges add one step inside move 2: **steelman the dissenter** in two or three sentences,
**name the cost** the commitment imposes and on whom, then hold or revise. Citing evidence as if it
settled a value is a mis-tag and caps the answer at 2 on criterion 10; evidence informs the cost,
it does not decide the commitment.

### Challenge-selection procedure

The procedure exists so that challenges are not chosen to be easy and so that they attack the brief
where the course predicts briefs are weak.

**Step 1: attack map.** Pair each [V] in s13 with the case and the reading-list dissenter that most
directly oppose it. Examples: "shared tests are worth a slower cycle" pairs with k02 and Dixit's
partial-metric displacement argument (`04` ch 14); "the boundary excludes contractors, and that is
acceptable" pairs with k20 and `09`'s warning that durability is not fairness; "members own the
rules" pairs with k19 and `04`'s subgame-perfect sanction design.

**Step 2: draw four cases at random** from k01-k24 under four constraints:

- at least two the learner did **not** answer during the course (unseen pool: k02, k03, k06, k07,
  k10, k12, k13, k17, k20, k21, k22, k23, k24);
- at least one **practice-scale** and at least one **system-scale** primary tension. Practice-scale
  tensions concern a single behaviour or norm: t01, t04, t05, t07, t10, t11, t13. System-scale
  tensions concern the group's structure, boundary, accountability or model of the people: t02, t03,
  t06, t08, t09, t12, t14;
- no two cases share a primary tension;
- redraw a case whose primary tension the brief marks "not binding here" only if another unseen
  case remains; otherwise keep it, since a case that attacks a "not binding" verdict is the best
  attack there is.

**Step 3: draw two value attacks** from the step-1 map. A co-planner, if available, writes each in
the dissenter's voice and reveals it only at the seal; otherwise the learner draws and writes the
steelman before the answer.

**Step 4: transplant.** Rewrite each case in one or two sentences so it happens in the learner's
group with the learner's people, keeping the primary tension. k12 (architecture decision records
stuck at a low share) in a community becomes "the moderation-log template has been used by a small
fraction of moderators for a year".

**Step 5: seal and answer.** Write all six down before answering any; no reordering after the seal.
Forty-five minutes for all six. An unanswered challenge scores 1 on criterion 10; a short answer with
five moves beats a long one with four.

**Worked draw.** The group is a forty-person open-source project with a twelve-person reviewer
guild. Shuffling the ids gives k21 (documentation norm never crossed the liaison; t09,
system-scale, unseen), k07 (forty saw the alarming post, nobody acted; t11, practice-scale,
unseen), k16 (quarterly rotation to spread the culture; t04, seen on R4) and k11 (weekly demo dies
from twelve to three; t04, seen on R3). k11 is redrawn because k16 holds t04; the redraw gives k22
(show-of-hands vote after the two most senior say yes; unseen). The four cover t09, t11, t04 and
k22's tension, with two unseen and both scales, so the draw stands. Step 3 draws "review quality is
worth a slower merge" attacked in Dixit's voice, and "contributors outside the guild may not vote on
review rules" attacked in Ostrom's voice with `09` ch 3 principle 3. Step 4 transplants k21 as "the
test-line norm never reached the docs team, whose only tie to the guild is one liaison". Seal.

Note on ids: seed 4d tagged k22 with a proposed t13 that covered cascades; under `tensions.md` k22
is an informational cascade (t11). Until `casebook.md` exists, identify a case's primary tension
from its book chapter.

### What the defense is not

It is not a recital. "My brief does not address this; here is what I would add in s9 and the
observation that would show me wrong" can earn a 4, because it makes all five moves honestly.
Reciting the tension file's rule about someone else's group cannot earn more than 2, because moves
2 and 3 never touch the brief. Nor is it a debate to win: revising after a steelmanned value attack
scores higher than holding without naming the cost.

## Deliverable 3: The first 90 days

One page plus a driver diagram, implementing v6.1: the loop from `unified-model.md` section 4 run
once at practice scale and set up to run again. Six parts:

1. **Year-one aim.** What will be different, for whom, by how much, by when. "What" and "for whom"
   are [V]; "how much" is an [H] with a number the learner expects to be wrong and a stated
   direction of error. One sentence.
2. **Driver diagram.** Aim; two to four primary drivers (the layers that must move: stability,
   selection, signal, expectations); secondary drivers; change ideas at the leaves. **A measure at
   each level**: outcome on the aim, process on each driver, practical on each change idea. Each
   change idea tagged [E] for its mechanism and [H] for its effect here, or [V] where it is a goal
   in disguise.
3. **The first three small tests.** Each: the change; an owner by name; the written prediction with
   a number; the practical measure, source and return time in days; the balancing measure that
   would show harm or gaming; start and review dates; the adopt/adapt/abandon rule written before
   the test starts. **At least one test targets an s12 [H] by id.** Tests run at the smallest scale
   where the game is the same (one cluster, or k from s5), never the whole population. The first
   test is the cheapest change that produces both information and standing: a change members can
   see was chosen by them.
4. **Launch of the collective-choice routine.** The recurring forum in which those bound by the
   rules can change them: cadence, convener, what it may change (operational rules) and may not
   (boundaries, which need the backstop), how a decision becomes public (the s11 signal), and the
   date of its first revision. This is the reference pattern's "development routine": what develops
   is the rule set and the group's ability to revise it.
5. **Three mechanisms observed personally, weekly**, each with "good" in one sentence and the
   two-week no-show action. For example: "the first sanction is alive: good is one public 'please
   add the test line' comment a week from someone other than me; none in two weeks, I ask the forum
   why"; "the lone cooperator is not worse off: good is early adopters' cycle time at the guild
   median; slower for two weeks, I lower the lone-cooperator cost before anything else".
6. **Days 30, 60, 90.** For each: what must be true (a measured statement) and what if not (return
   to station 1 because the game is unstable; to station 2 because expectations did not move; or
   scale to the next cluster because both moved). Day 90 names what would make the learner abandon
   the brief's main hypothesis, by s12 id.

A plan that runs the whole group at once, has no balancing measure, or whose tests have no written
prediction caps criterion 9 at 2.

## Rubric

Each criterion is scored 0-4. The reference pattern scores 1-4; this course adds 0 for "absent", so
an absent section is distinguishable from a wrong one.

- **4** = an expert practitioner would sign it: the working parts are named (numbers, mechanisms,
  the switch signal, the register entry) and agree with the rest of the brief.
- **3** = sound; gaps a colleague catches in one read.
- **2** = the right words without the working parts ("a balanced approach", "we will iterate",
  "graduated sanctions" with no ladder).
- **1** = wrong, contradicted elsewhere, or a rejected folk theory endorsed untagged.
- **0** = absent.

**Pass:** 32/40, no criterion below 2. **World-class:** 36/40 with a 4 on each of **1 (the game is
drawn before the fix), 2 (equilibrium selection respects risk dominance), 3 (expectations measured,
not assumed) and 7 (cluster logic used correctly)**. These four separate a brief built on how
cooperation works from one built on how cooperation plans usually look. An ordinary plan already has
sanctions, owners and metrics in list form; it never has a drawn game with the defection question
answered, a selection plan that starts from the risk-dominant default, a measured pair of
expectations, or a seed set inside the resisting cluster conditional on success-copying. Those are
the loop stations where the course's mechanism is the only reason to do the step, and they are what
ledger rule 1 and rule 3 hang on. Criteria 5, 6 and 9 a careful manager can reach; 8 and 10 are the
course's discipline, graded hard, but a 3 on them is compatible with world-class practice.

### Criteria 1-7: domain criteria, in level order

The order runs up the building: Mechanisms (1, 2), Order norms (3, 4), Order institutions (5, 6),
Order networks (7). Origins has no criterion of its own; it is tested inside 7 (which copying bias
the seeding assumes) and 8 (explanation is a weak channel).

#### Criterion 1. The game is drawn before the fix

*Where to look:* s2, then s1 for the boundary the payoffs assume; every fix in s7-s10 checked
against s2's fix set. Law 1.

- **4.** A matrix or tree per target behaviour with players, options, ordinal payoffs, order and
  information; the defection question answered in writing with the group's own payoffs; the shape
  named and tagged [H]; licensed and forbidden fix sets both stated; every lever in s7-s10 traceable
  to the licensed set; at least one candidate classified as "not a game: arithmetic" or "a
  convention, self-enforcing" and dropped from the fix list; for a population, a binary-choice
  diagram or its verbal equivalent in s5.
- **3.** Drawn and classified, but one later lever belongs to the other shape's fix set (assurance
  devices in a dilemma, sanctions in a stag hunt) with no argument, or payoffs are asserted rather
  than drawn.
- **2.** "Prisoner's dilemma" or "stag hunt" appears without the defection question answered; or the
  fix is stated before s2 and s2 is written to justify it. Any fix before the diagnosis caps here.
- **1.** The classification tagged [E] (rule 13); "repetition solves it" untagged (rule 15); or a
  villain in the matrix (a payoff row that only makes sense if one member is assumed malicious, `03`
  ch 5).
- **0.** No s2.

#### Criterion 2. The equilibrium-selection plan respects risk dominance

*Where to look:* s7, with s5 (threshold, k) and s6 (weak-link unit size). Laws 2 and 3; rules 2 and
13; t04.

- **4.** The regime is diagnosed (mixing, learning rule, talk, size, history) and tagged [H]; the plan
  names the safe outcome the group drifts to absent intervention; correlation devices listed in cost
  order with the lone-cooperator cost first and stated before and after; explicit pacing (grow from a
  core with visible history, small stable units while the norm is young, no random rotation); a
  group-specific switch signal toward risk dominance; any weak-link unit capped at a handful or its
  aggregation rule changed; "sweeten the joint payoff" absent or ranked last with the reason.
- **3.** Devices and pacing present, but the lone-cooperator cost is not stated before and after, or
  the regime lacks the learning rule, or the switch signal is generic.
- **2.** "Build trust and momentum" without a device; raising the joint reward as the lead lever; or
  rotating people across units to spread the practice.
- **1.** "The efficient equilibrium is the rational one" or "once people see it pays they will
  switch" untagged (rule 2); non-adoption attributed to insufficient reward or irrationality
  (rule 16).
- **0.** No s7.

#### Criterion 3. Expectations are measured, not assumed

*Where to look:* s8 (instrument, sample, date, results), s1 (reference network), s14 (re-measure).
Law 4; rules 10, 11; t13; loop station 2.

- **4.** The four questions written out for a specific behaviour, not a value; sample drawn from the
  s1 reference network, dated, with results or a planned date; private attitudes collected so the
  pluralistic-ignorance gap is computable; the reading caveated as design-sensitive (Bicchieri and
  Xiao 2009 cited for direction only); a dated re-measure with all three outcomes (both moved;
  behaviour only; expectations only) mapped to a loop station; every norm assumed conditional until
  it survives a collapse of expectations (rule 10).
- **3.** Instrument dated, but the sample is the org chart, or the re-measure is missing, or attitudes
  are collected without the two expectation questions.
- **2.** "We surveyed buy-in" stands in for the measurement; or measurement planned with no date and
  no instrument.
- **1.** Expectations asserted from the learner's own belief and tagged [E]; or an expectations
  campaign run across clusters that cannot see each other (t13 never-clause).
- **0.** No measurement.

#### Criterion 4. Norms carry both expectation types

*Where to look:* s8 register rows; s11 norm test. Rules 11, 24; t10; law 5.

- **4.** Every rule classified with the four-way taxonomy and the classification argued; for every
  social norm the empirical and the normative expectation as two sentences, each with the mechanism
  that creates or repairs it; descriptive and injunctive signals paired in every message, none
  advertising low adoption; at least one rule correctly identified as a convention or a descriptive
  regularity that is not a norm; conventions used wherever a coordination good allows (`08`
  implications).
- **3.** Main norm complete, but a secondary rule called a "norm" without classification, or one
  message pairs signals inconsistently.
- **2.** "Social norm" used for "what most people do" (rule 11); or normative expectations named but
  created by nothing beyond a values statement.
- **1.** Any message of the form "too many people still do X" (law 5, caps at 1); or a values
  statement presented as the norm intervention and tagged [E].
- **0.** No register.

#### Criterion 5. Sanctions are cheap and graduated; monitoring is a by-product

*Where to look:* s9 (ladder, monitoring, forum); s2 (bites only if a dilemma margin was found);
Deliverable 3 part 5. Law 9; rules 4, 30; t02; t12.

- **4.** A written ladder whose first step is small, quick, public and cheap for the sanctioner (a
  comment, an unapproved review, a name on a rota), escalation for repeat or flagrant cases, a stated
  top step; monitoring as a by-product of the work (visible commits, public logs, turn-taking) with
  no policing role nobody wants; a fast, local, cheap dispute forum; the sanctioner's cost stated and
  the first step cheaper than looking away; in a diagnosed stag hunt the ladder marked as backstop
  not engine; any metric's displaced unmeasured effort named and watched (t12); antisocial
  punishment named with its check.
- **3.** Ladder and by-product monitoring present, but sanctioner's cost or forum missing, or the top
  step undefined.
- **2.** "Graduated sanctions" with no steps; monitoring as a dashboard someone must maintain; a
  leaderboard with no crowding-out watch.
- **1.** A severe first response (ban, lockout, removal) untagged; the ladder justified by "people
  are evolved to punish cheats" tagged [E] (rule 4); a raise or bonus as the engine (rule 28).
- **0.** No sanction design where s2 found a dilemma.

#### Criterion 6. The brief names who has standing to change the rules

*Where to look:* s9 (standing, arena, backstop), s1 (boundary), Deliverable 3 part 4. Law 10; rule
29; t06; t14; m11 hinge Q3.

- **4.** For each class of rule (operational, collective-choice, boundary): who may change it, in what
  arena, on what cadence, recognized by whom outside the group; the first revision dated; the eight
  principles answered as questions with at least one "not binding here" and one "not known, test in
  s12"; the boundary's exclusion tagged [V] with the dissenter; imposed rules marked "complied with
  while enforced" with a budget line; for a digital or knowledge commons, the scope caveat and
  principles 1 and 2 reinterpreted as membership and contribution roles (C48).
- **3.** Standing named for operational rules and a backstop present, but the arena has no cadence
  or the exclusion is untagged.
- **2.** "The team owns its process" with no arena, cadence or recognition; or the principles listed
  as things to install.
- **1.** Rules written by the learner or an authority with no route to change them and tagged
  durable; principles cited as a recipe or as evidence of fairness (rule 29); self-governance assumed
  with no external recognition and claimed to hold.
- **0.** No standing decision, and Part A did not name it as the missing decision.

#### Criterion 7. The network seeding plan uses cluster logic correctly

*Where to look:* s10, s3 (copying bias), s7 (pacing). Laws 7 and 8; rules 3, 19, 33, 34, 36; t09;
t04's cluster clause.

- **4.** Clusters and bridges sketched; the behaviour classified as informational cascade, network
  effect or coordination on a graph, with the matching fix (collect private signals first; cross the
  tipping point; lower q or seed inside clusters); q estimated in words from the switching and
  lone-adopter costs; seed set inside the most cohesive resisting cluster, with the reason (it blocks
  only while none of its members has switched); bridges widened into parallel ties, not one liaison,
  because costly behaviour is a complex contagion; the learning-rule condition stated (clusters help
  only under success-copying), tied to s3 and to a plan for making success visible; awareness
  (crosses a weak tie) distinguished from adoption; similarity read as selection, not influence,
  absent longitudinal data.
- **3.** Seeded inside a cluster with bridges widened, but the copying kind unnamed, or the
  learning-rule condition asserted without s3, or q unestimated.
- **2.** "Find the influencers"; a single announcement to everyone; clusters treated as always good or
  always bad.
- **1.** "Six degrees" or "useful information comes from weak ties" as fact (rule 33); adoption
  expected across weak ties (rule 34); tipping attributed to charismatic people (rule 18).
- **0.** No network section.

### Criterion 8. Tagging discipline

*Where to look:* every section; s4, s12, s13; the v0 changelog; Part A. Law 12; rules 5-9, 20, 27,
40; SPEC tag definitions.

- **4.** Every declarative design sentence carries exactly one tag; compound claims split into "[E]
  for the mechanism, [H] for its effect here"; every [E] names its ledger slug or book file and

  findings caveated wherever they appear (cultural group selection, the collective-brain size effect,
  culture-drove-brains, evolved strong reciprocity, norm-based utility's superiority, gradual growth
  as one lab lineage, the 2-5 rule of thumb); s12 has at least eight [H] with tests and dates, s13
  at least six [V] with dissenters; the changelog shows at least eight substantive reversals with
  module and author attribution, one v0 position kept with reason, the free response rewritten
  beside the original, and the retake with calibration delta; nothing from the failed-replication
  list used.
- **3.** Complete, but one or two compound claims untagged, one [E] without its slug, or the
  changelog has fewer than eight reversals or no kept position (caps here).
- **2.** Most sentences tagged but "we believe" and "research shows" appear untagged; [E] used for a
  prescription about this group; registers thin (under five [H] or four [V]).
- **1.** Caps at 1 if any of these appears untagged: ego depletion, priming, power posing, watching
  eyes, the Stanford Prison Experiment, groupthink as a syndrome, IAT training, a large general
  stereotype-threat effect, Kitty Genovese as fact, "six degrees" as fact, "the efficient equilibrium
  is rational", cultural group selection as the basis of a design claim.
- **0.** No tags.

### Criterion 9. The measurement plan includes a balancing measure

*Where to look:* s14; Deliverable 3 parts 2, 3, 5, 6. Loop stations 4 and 5; rule 27;
`rogers-paradox`; t12.

- **4.** Every lever in s14 has a prediction with a number written before the test, a practical
  measure with a named source and a return time in days, and a balancing measure that would show
  harm or gaming (cycle time, deleted tests, displaced unmeasured effort, exits, appeals over the
  group's head); the driver diagram has a measure at each level; the three tests run at the smallest
  scale where the game is the same; at least one test targets an s12 [H] by id; the three re-measure
  outcomes each trigger a named action and the abandon observation is named; lab and model numbers
  used for direction only, the group's own measurement the only point estimate (rule 27); day
  30/60/90 "what if not" branches return to a named loop station.
- **3.** Predictions and measures dated, but one test lacks a balancing measure, or the abandon rule
  is written after the fact, or one driver lacks a process measure.
- **2.** "We will track engagement" with no prediction; the balancing measure repeats the practical
  one; the test is the whole group.
- **1.** A lab or model number used as the group's prediction ("cooperation will double, Dawes
  1977"); a direction with no return time; a plan that contradicts s2 (measures assurance in a
  diagnosed dilemma with no sanction measure).

### Criterion 10. The design survives the Gintis-style critique of its own rationality assumptions

*Where to look:* s11 (signal, norm test, BPC statement, layers); Part A and Part B. Rules 1, 14, 16,
31; t03; t11; law 11; `11` critical assessment.

The critique is the one `11` makes of game theory and the one the course makes of `11`: "members
will cooperate because it is rational" assumes common knowledge of conjectures that nothing in the
design supplies; "members are not rational, so we nudge" throws away the consistency the levers rely
on; tagging the BPC model or the correlated-equilibrium framing as [E] is the mistake the ledger
names in C55-C58.

- **4.** s11 names the public signal for each coordination point, who is credible enough to send it
  and what costly action makes them so; the two-part norm test is answered; the BPC statement says
  which beliefs about each other the design assumes, which preferences it counts on (with the caveat
  that no single social-preference model is [E]) and which constraints it changes, tagged [H] as
  bookkeeping (t11), not [E] as psychology; every element mapped to one of the four layers and no
  layer unaddressed without a reason; "rational" never contrasted with "fair" (rule 16); Nash never
  treated as a prediction (rule 14); Part A finds at least one real tag error or gap and names the
  most important missing decision; all six Part B answers make all five moves with group-specific
  switch signals, both value attacks steelmanned with the cost named, and at least one answer changes
  the brief (v6.1 logged).
- **3.** Sound, but one coordination point has no signal, or the BPC statement omits beliefs, or one
  Part B answer has four moves, or one value attack is answered with evidence rather than a steelman.
- **2.** "Clear communication" as the signal; defense answers that are correct generalities about
  other groups; Part A finds nothing.
- **1.** "Once people see it is rational they will cooperate" untagged; "people are irrational" as
  the premise; Gintis presented as "the unification" with correlated equilibrium tagged [E] (rule
  31); a lab result cited as settling a value.
- **0.** No s11 and no defense.

## Worked exemplar: what a 4 looks like

Excerpts from a hypothetical learner's v6 for a forty-person open-source project with a
twelve-person reviewer guild; the target behaviour is "every qualifying pull request carries a test
line". Every number below is the learner's own estimate or count, not a ledger figure.

### Criterion 1 (s2), excerpt

> **The game, for a guild member deciding whether to write the test line.** Players: me and the other
> eleven reviewers as "the rest". Options: write (W) or skip (S). Ordinal payoffs, my estimate from
> last quarter's review log [H]: rest write, I write: a suite I can rely on, minus about an hour (3);
> rest write, I skip: the suite, hour saved (4); rest skip, I write: the hour spent on a broken suite
> (0); rest skip, I skip: no suite, hour saved (1). Order: simultaneous within a sprint, repeated
> across sprints among the same twelve. Information: my choice is in the review log within a day.
>
> **Defection question.** Does a member gain by skipping when everyone else writes? Yes, 4 over 3.
> At the sprint margin this is a dilemma, not a stag hunt [H for the payoffs; the distinction is
> exact, `04` ch 4, rule 13]. It repeats with fast detection among a fixed twelve, so a cooperative
> equilibrium exists at layer 1 if detection is used [E for existence, `04` ch 10; H that the guild
> coordinates on it, rule 15].
>
> **Licensed fix set:** payoffs (lower the hour), repetition (present), detection and sanction (a
> review line, an unapproved review). **Forbidden as the engine:** assurance devices alone, because
> talk decays where defection dominates [E lab, H here; rule 12]. They stay as the layer-4 launch.
>
> **Not a game:** v0 said "reviewers are too slow to also write tests". By my count the guild's
> capacity exceeds the qualifying-PR rate, so this is not an arithmetic constraint [H, re-check
> monthly, s12-03]. Dropped.

*Why it earns a 4.* All five elements of the game are drawn; the defection question is answered with
the group's own numbers and drives the shape; the shape is [H] and the distinction [E] (rule 13);
both fix sets are stated and the forbidden one is placed at its layer rather than discarded; a v0
belief is tested against arithmetic and dropped; no villain sits in the matrix.

*What would drop it to 3.* Omitting the "not a game" check, or writing "it is a repeated PD so
tit-for-tat holds it" (rule 15), or payoffs with no review-log source. "This is a PD" with no
defection question would drop it to 2.

### Criterion 3 (s8), excerpt

> **Elicitation, run 14 September, guild only** (the s1 reference network: the twelve who review each
> other's PRs; docs and drive-by contributors do not watch the guild's log and are excluded [H that
> this boundary is right; s12-05]). Behaviour: "adding a test line to a qualifying PR", not "caring
> about quality". Four questions answered privately, plus private attitude.
>
> - What do you do on a qualifying PR? 5 of 12 usually add the line.
> - What do most guild members do? 9 of 12 believe most skip.
> - What do most guild members think you should do? 3 of 12 believe most think you should add it;
>   7 said "nobody minds either way".
> - What happens if you skip? 11 of 12: nothing.
> - Privately, should qualifying PRs carry a test line? 10 of 12: yes.
>
> **Reading [H, design-sensitive; Bicchieri and Xiao 2009 for the direction that expectations of
> skipping suppress writing even under pro-writing messages].** Empirical expectation: most skip.
> Normative expectation: absent. Private attitude: strongly pro-writing. This is a descriptive norm
> of skipping held up by pluralistic ignorance, not a social norm of skipping, and no social norm of
> writing exists yet. The gap (10 of 12 privately for, 3 of 12 believing others are) is what to
> publish, from a guild member, not from me [H, s12-06].
>
> **Re-measure:** same five questions, same twelve, 26 October and 7 December (s14). Behaviour up and
> both expectations moved: extend to the docs team through the two parallel ties in s10. Behaviour
> up, expectations unchanged: the change rides on the review-line sanction and decays if it lapses;
> put the forum's first revision on the agenda. Expectations moved, behaviour unchanged: s2 was wrong
> about the hour; lower it before anything else.

*Why it earns a 4.* A specific behaviour; the reference network as sample with the exclusion tagged
[H]; empirical and normative expectations separated and each read; private attitude makes the gap
computable; the taxonomy yields "descriptive norm plus pluralistic ignorance", which licenses a
different lever than "social norm of skipping" would; a dated re-measure whose three outcomes each
return to a loop station. Counts are the learner's own; the one citation is for direction.

*What would drop it to 3.* Sampling the whole project, omitting the private-attitude question, or an
undated re-measure. Replacing the four questions with "how much do you agree that tests matter?"
would drop it to 2.
