# Capstone: the Cooperation brief, the defense, the first 90 days

Status: written against `SPEC.md`, `course.json`, `synthesis/learning-design.md` (module ids m0..m11,
brief sections s1..s14 from seed 4c, casebook one-liners k01..k24 from seed 4d, capstone seed 4f),
`synthesis/claims-ledger.md` (section 5 binding; section 4 for every number), `synthesis/tensions.md`
(t01..t14, that file's headings as of writing) and `synthesis/unified-model.md` (the twelve laws and
the five-station loop). `synthesis/casebook.md` and `synthesis/design-brief-template.md` did not
exist when this was written; the ids used here are the learning-design seeds those files must keep.
The reconciliation log at the end records every departure.

The capstone asks one question: **could you diagnose why a real group cooperates, coordinates or
fails to, design the norms, incentives, network structure and institutions that fix it, defend that
design under attack, and run its first ninety days as a learning system?** Everything the course did
was preparation for these three sittings. The brief is the design; the defense is the design under
pressure; the ninety-day plan is the design turned into a loop that can tell you it was wrong.

## Deliverable 1: Cooperation brief v6

Use the section skeleton fixed in `learning-design.md` seed 4c (s1-s14); `design-brief-template.md`
is authoritative once written and keeps these ids. The brief is about one real group the learner can
observe from the inside: a team, a community, a platform, or a commons. It is 3,000-4,000 words, and
every declarative design sentence carries one tag. The minimum contents list below says what must be
present in each section; **a missing item caps the matching rubric criterion at 2** (the criterion
number is in brackets).

- **s1 The group and its boundary.** Who is in and who is out, stated as a membership rule not a
  headcount; what is shared; whether it is subtractable (a commons) or not (a knowledge or
  coordination good); the reference network, meaning the set of people whose behaviour members
  actually watch, which is not the org chart; the exclusion the boundary creates, tagged [V].
  Missing boundary or missing reference network caps criterion 1 or 3 respectively. [1, 3]
- **s2 Diagnosis: the games being played.** For the one or two behaviours the brief is about: the
  players, options, payoffs (ordinal is fine), order of moves and information, drawn as a matrix or
  tree; the written answer to the one agreed test, "does a member gain by defecting when everyone
  else cooperates?"; the resulting shape (dilemma, stag hunt, chicken, pure coordination, or "not a
  game: an accounting constraint"); the fix set that shape licenses and the fix set it forbids; the
  classification tagged [H]. A fix stated before this section or contradicting it caps criterion 1
  at 2. [1]
- **s3 The collective brain.** Who learns what from whom; which practices spread by modelling and
  which need teaching (ledger rule 8); the single points of failure (skills held by one person);
  which copying bias the group runs on (success, prestige, conformity), because criterion 7 depends
  on it. [7]
- **s4 Situational audit and folk-psychology check.** The dispositional explanations the learner
  arrived with ("the coasters", "the toxic reviewer") re-read situationally; the levers struck
  through because they sit on the failed-replication list (ledger rule 20); the levers kept from the
  robust list. Any reliance on a struck-through lever elsewhere in the brief caps criterion 8 at 1.
  [8]
- **s5 Threshold and tipping map.** Whether behaviour is contingent on how many others do it; the
  rough shape of the threshold distribution; the minimum viable coalition k; any arithmetic
  constraint (a musical-chairs fact no persuasion can change). [2]
- **s6 Population of types and fairness baseline.** The estimated mix of conditional cooperators,
  self-interested members and the tail, stated as a hypothesis with the ledger's caveat that the
  distribution is rough; the fairness baseline members expect; whether any process is weak-link
  (the minimum contributor sets the outcome) and the size of the unit whose minimum matters. [2]
- **s7 Equilibrium-selection plan.** The regime the group is in (random mixing vs small stable
  neighbourhoods; majority-copying vs success-copying; talk possible or not); the correlation
  devices chosen, in cost order (lower the lone-cooperator cost, leader names the target, public
  opt-in pledges, gradual growth from a core with visible history, stable small units); the pacing
  rule; the lone-cooperator cost before and after the change; the switch signal toward risk
  dominance. "Raise the reward for joint success" as the lead lever caps criterion 2 at 2. [2]
- **s8 Norm register.** Every rule the brief relies on, classified with Bicchieri's four-way
  taxonomy (descriptive, convention, social, moral); for each social norm the empirical expectation
  and the normative expectation stated separately; the elicitation instrument (the four questions:
  what do you do, what do most here do, what do most here think you should do, what happens if you
  don't) with its sample and date; the measured or to-be-measured gap between private attitude and
  perceived norm; cues; candidate trendsetters chosen by position and sensitivity, not charisma.
  [3, 4]
- **s9 Institutional design.** The eight principles used as questions with an answer per principle
  including "not binding here"; the sanction ladder starting with a small, quick, public first step;
  monitoring as a by-product of the work; the dispute forum; **standing: who may change which
  rules, in what arena, recognized by whom**; the external backstop; the boundary's cost as [V].
  [5, 6]
- **s10 Network map and seeding plan.** A sketch of clusters and bridges in the reference network;
  which kind of copying the target behaviour is (informational cascade, network effect, or
  coordination on a graph, ledger rule 19); the threshold q = b/(a+b) estimated in words for the
  target behaviour; the seed set, placed inside the resisting cluster; bridges to widen; whether
  the learning rule in s3 lets clusters help. [7]
- **s11 The signal and the BPC statement.** The public cue that tells each member which equilibrium
  is in play (rota, written rule, dashboard, default); the two-part norm test (can each member
  state what the norm requires of them and of others; does anyone gain by deviating and is there a
  cheap sanction); the BPC statement, meaning what the design assumes about members' beliefs about
  each other, their preferences including fairness and status, and their constraints; which of the
  four layers (stability, selection, signal, expectations) each design element addresses. [10]
- **s12 Open hypotheses register.** Every [H] in the brief with its test, its measure and its
  date; at least eight entries. [8]
- **s13 Value commitments register.** Every [V] with the dissenter from the reading list who would
  object and the cost the learner accepts; at least six entries. [8]
- **s14 Measurement plan and ninety-day predictions.** For each lever: the prediction with a
  number, the practical measure and where it comes from, the balancing measure that would show
  harm or gaming, the return time, the abandon rule. [9]
- **Appendix: v0 changelog.** Line-by-line re-tag of the m0 page against v6: every reversal with
  the module and author that caused it; every v0 position kept, with the reason; the rewritten
  baseline free response next to the day-1 version; the diagnostic retake with the calibration
  delta. Fewer than eight substantive reversals or no kept position caps criterion 8 at 3. [8]

Length discipline: s2, s7, s8, s9 and s10 carry the design and should take about two-thirds of the
words; s3-s6 are diagnostic and can be tight; s12-s14 are tables.

## Deliverable 2: The defense

The defense has two parts written in one sitting. **Part A** answers the `course.json` critique
prompt against the learner's own v6 in 400-600 words: (1) every tag that is arguably wrong, in both
directions; (2) every contradiction with what the course teaches, checked against the six named
items (game before fix; both expectation types; Ostrom's principles; network structure shapes
diffusion; social preferences real but conditional; risk dominance beats payoff dominance under
uncertainty); (3) inconsistencies between sections; (4) the single most important missing decision.
The learner writes this as the critic, not as the author; a Part A that finds nothing is scored 1 on
criterion 10 because m11's own hinge says the standing decision and the signal are missing from most
briefs, and a v6 that has neither gap nor a sentence showing why it has neither is not yet defended.

**Part B** answers six sealed challenges: four casebook cases transplanted into the learner's group
and two value attacks on the learner's own [V] commitments. Each answer is 200-300 words, or the six
together are a 20-minute recorded talk. Every answer makes the same five moves, in order:

1. **Name the tension**, by id and name (for example "t04, risk dominance vs payoff dominance"),
   and say which pole the challenge is pushing toward.
2. **Apply the rule to the case and to the brief**, citing the section: which IF branch of the
   tension's rule the group is in, and what s7 (or whichever section) already says about it.
3. **State the switch signal**: the number or observation that would tell the learner they chose the
   wrong pole, taken from the tension file and made specific to the group ("effort falls round on
   round in a unit above about a dozen" becomes "review turnaround in the twelve-person guild
   lengthens for three consecutive weeks").
4. **Say what you do not know, tagged.** Which claim in the answer is [E] for the mechanism and [H]
   for this group; which is [V].
5. **Say what changes in the brief, or why nothing does.** At least one of the six answers must
   change the brief; the change is logged as v6.1 with a one-line entry in s12 or s13.

Value challenges follow the same five moves with one addition inside move 2: **steelman the
dissenter** from the reading list in two or three sentences (the strongest version of the objection,
not the easiest), **name the cost** the learner's commitment imposes and on whom, then hold or
revise. Citing evidence as if it settled a value is a mis-tag and drops the answer to 2 on
criterion 10; the evidence can inform the cost, not decide the commitment.

### Challenge-selection procedure

The purpose of the procedure is that the challenges are not chosen by the learner to be easy, and
that they attack the brief where the course predicts briefs are weak.

**Step 1: the attack map.** For each [V] in s13, pair it with the casebook case and the reading-list
dissenter that most directly oppose it. Examples of pairings: "shared tests are worth a slower
cycle" pairs with k02 (quality first announced, broken feature shipped) and Dixit's partial-metric
displacement argument (`04` ch 14); "the boundary excludes contractors and that is acceptable" pairs
with k20 (staff-only moderation) and `09`'s own warning that durability is not fairness; "members
own the rules" pairs with k19 (automatic lockout quotas) and `04`'s subgame-perfect sanction design.

**Step 2: draw four cases at random**, from the seed list k01-k24, under four constraints:

- at least two cases the learner did **not** answer during the course (the retrieval schedule
  used k01, k04, k05, k08, k09, k11, k14, k15, k16, k18, k19, so the unseen pool is k02, k03, k06,
  k07, k10, k12, k13, k17, k20, k21, k22, k23, k24);
- at least one **practice-scale** tension and at least one **system-scale** tension among the four
  primary tensions. For this course, practice-scale tensions are those about a single behaviour or
  norm: t01, t04, t05, t07, t10, t11, t13. System-scale tensions are those about the group's
  structure, boundary, accountability or model of the people: t02, t03, t06, t08, t09, t12, t14;
- no two cases share a primary tension;
- redraw any case whose primary tension is one the learner's brief explicitly says is "not binding
  here" in s2 or s9, unless it is the only unseen case left, in which case keep it: a case that
  attacks a "not binding" verdict is the best attack there is.

**Step 3: draw two value attacks.** Pick two entries from the step-1 attack map at random. If a
co-planner is available, they write the attack in the dissenter's voice without showing the learner
the [V] they chose until the seal; if not, the learner draws and writes the steelman before writing
the answer.

**Step 4: transplant.** Rewrite each case in one or two sentences so it happens in the learner's own
group with the learner's own people, keeping the case's primary tension intact. k12 (architecture
decision records stuck at a low share) in a community becomes "the moderation-log template has been
used by a small fraction of moderators for a year".

**Step 5: seal and answer.** Write the six challenges down before answering any; do not reorder or
swap after sealing. Forty-five minutes for all six. A challenge left unanswered at the timebox is
scored 1 on criterion 10; a short answer with all five moves beats a long answer with four.

**Worked example of a draw.** The learner's group is a forty-person open-source project with a
twelve-person reviewer guild. Step 1 produces four [V] pairings. Step 2 draws, by shuffling the
twenty-four ids: k21 (documentation norm that never crossed the liaison; t09, system-scale, unseen),
k07 (forty people saw the alarming post, nobody acted; t11, practice-scale, unseen), k16 (quarterly
rotation to spread the culture; t04 with t09 secondary, practice-scale, seen on R4), k11 (weekly
demo dies from twelve to three; t04, seen on R3). k11 is redrawn because k16 already holds t04 as
primary; the redraw gives k22 (show-of-hands vote after the two most senior say yes; t13 as the
seed lists it, but see the note below; practice-scale, unseen). The four now cover t09, t11, t04
and t13 with two unseen and both scales, so the draw stands. Step 3 draws "review quality is worth a
slower merge" attacked in Dixit's voice, and "contributors outside the guild may not vote on review
rules" attacked in Ostrom's voice with `09` ch 3 principle 3. Step 4 transplants k21 as "the
test-line norm the guild adopted never reached the docs team, whose only tie to the guild is one
liaison". Step 5 seals all six.

Note on ids: the seed list in `learning-design.md` 4d was written against a proposed tension list;
`tensions.md` is canonical and its t13 is "shifting expectations vs changing structure" while the
seed's k22 was tagged with the seed's t13 (informational cascades, now under t11 and `10` ch 16).
Until `casebook.md` is written, use the case's book chapter to identify the primary tension: k22 is
an informational cascade (t11, with t13's structure branch as the fix), and the draw above treats it
so.

### What the defense is not

It is not a recital. "My brief does not address this; here is what I would add in s9 and here is the
observation that would show me wrong" can earn a 4, because it makes all five moves honestly.
Reciting the tension file's rule about someone else's group cannot earn more than 2, because moves 2
and 3 are missing the brief. Nor is it a debate to win: an answer that revises the brief after a
steelmanned value attack scores higher than one that holds without naming the cost.

## Deliverable 3: The first 90 days

One page plus a driver diagram, implementing v6.1. The plan is the loop from `unified-model.md`
section 4 run once at practice scale and set up to run again. It must contain the six parts below.

1. **Year-one aim.** What will be different, for whom, by how much, by when. The "what" and "for
   whom" are [V] choices and are tagged so; the "how much" is an [H] with a number the learner
   expects to be wrong and a stated direction of error. One sentence.

2. **Driver diagram.** Aim at the left; two to four primary drivers (the layers that must move: the
   game's stability, equilibrium selection, the signal, expectations); secondary drivers under each;
   change ideas at the leaves. **A measure at each level**: an outcome measure on the aim, a process
   measure on each driver, a practical measure on each change idea. Every change idea is tagged [E]
   for its mechanism and [H] for its effect here, or [V] where it is a goal in disguise. Draw it
   with the course's mermaid convention or by hand; a list with indentation is acceptable if each
   node carries its measure.

3. **The first three small tests.** Each has: the change; the owner (a name); the written prediction
   with a number; the practical measure and its return time (days, not quarters); the balancing
   measure that would show harm or gaming; the start and review dates; the adopt/adapt/abandon
   rule stated before the test starts. **At least one of the three tests an [H] from the s12
   register**, quoting its id. Tests run at the smallest scale where the game is the same: one
   cluster, or the minimum viable coalition k from s5, never the whole population. The first test
   is the cheapest change that produces both information and standing: a change members can see
   was chosen by them.

4. **Launch of the collective-choice routine.** The recurring forum in which those bound by the
   rules can change them (learning-design m8 and m11 call this the decision learners skip most):
   its cadence, who convenes it, what it may change (operational rules) and what it may not
   (boundaries, which need the external backstop), how a decision becomes public (the signal from
   s11), and the date of the first revision it will make. This is the "development routine" of the
   reference pattern, adapted: what develops is the rule set and the group's ability to revise it.

5. **Three mechanisms the learner will personally observe weekly.** Each with "good" in one
   sentence and the two-week no-show action. Examples of the kind expected: "the first sanction is
   used and is small: good looks like at least one public 'please add the test line' comment a week
   from someone who is not me; if none in two weeks, the ladder is not alive and I ask the guild
   forum why"; "the elicitation gap is closing: good looks like the monthly four-question pulse
   showing the empirical expectation moving toward the measured behaviour; if not in two weeks of
   data, the publication was not from a credible source and I change the messenger"; "the lone
   cooperator is not worse off: good looks like the earliest adopters' cycle time within the guild
   median; if they are visibly slower for two weeks, I lower the lone-cooperator cost before doing
   anything else".

6. **Days 30, 60, 90.** For each checkpoint: what must be true (a measured statement), and what
   if not (the branch: return to station 1 because the game is unstable; return to station 2
   because expectations did not move; scale to the next cluster because both moved). Day 90 also
   names what would make the learner abandon the main hypothesis of the brief, quoting the s12 id.

A plan that runs the whole group at once, has no balancing measure, or whose tests have no written
prediction before they start caps criterion 9 at 2.

## Rubric

Each criterion is scored 0-4. The reference pattern scores 1-4; this course adds 0 for "section
absent or not attempted", so that an absent section is distinguishable from a wrong one. Descriptors:

- **4** = an expert practitioner would sign it: the working parts are named (numbers, mechanisms,
  the switch signal, the register entry) and they agree with the rest of the brief.
- **3** = sound; the gaps are ones a colleague catches in one read (a part asserted but not visible
  elsewhere, one missing number).
- **2** = the right words without the working parts ("we will iterate", "a balanced approach",
  "graduated sanctions" with no ladder).
- **1** = wrong, contradicted elsewhere in the brief, or a rejected folk theory endorsed untagged.
- **0** = absent.

**Pass:** 32/40 with no criterion below 2. **World-class:** 36/40 with a 4 on each of the four named
criteria: **1 (the game is drawn before the fix), 2 (equilibrium selection respects risk
dominance), 3 (expectations measured, not assumed), 7 (cluster logic used correctly)**. These four
separate a brief built on how cooperation works from a brief built on how cooperation plans usually
look. An ordinary plan already has sanctions, owners and metrics, in list form, and a competent
manager writes them without the course; it never has a drawn game with the defection question
answered, a selection plan that starts from the risk-dominant default, a measured pair of
expectations, or a seed set placed inside the resisting cluster on the condition that people copy
success. Those four are the four stations of the loop where the course's mechanism is the only
reason to do the step, and they are the four the ledger's spine (rule 1: stability, selection,
signal, expectations; rule 3: the cluster condition) hangs on. Criteria 5, 6 and 9 can be reached by
a careful manager; 8 and 10 are the course's discipline and are graded hard, but a 3 on them is
compatible with world-class practice.

### Criteria 1-7: domain criteria, in level order

The order runs up the building: Mechanisms (1, 2), Order norms (3, 4), Order institutions (5, 6),
Order networks (7). Origins has no criterion of its own; it is tested inside 7 (which copying bias
the seeding plan assumes) and 8 (the "explanation is a weak channel" rule).

#### Criterion 1. The game is drawn before the fix

*Where to look:* s2, then s1 for the boundary the payoffs assume; check every fix in s7-s10 against
s2's fix set. Law 1.

- **4.** A matrix or tree for each target behaviour with players, options, ordinal payoffs, order
  and information; the defection question answered in writing with the group's own payoffs; the
  shape named and tagged [H]; the licensed fix set and the forbidden fix set both stated; every
  lever in s7-s10 traceable to the licensed set; at least one candidate behaviour classified as
  "not a game: arithmetic" or "a convention, self-enforcing" and dropped from the fix list; for a
  large population, a Schelling binary-choice diagram or its verbal equivalent in s5.
- **3.** The game is drawn and classified, but one lever in a later section belongs to the other
  shape's fix set (assurance devices in a dilemma, or sanctions in a stag hunt) with no argument,
  or the payoffs are asserted rather than drawn.
- **2.** The words "prisoner's dilemma" or "stag hunt" appear without the defection question
  answered; or the fix is stated in s1 or in a preamble and s2 is written to justify it. Any fix
  proposed before the diagnosis caps here.
- **1.** The classification is tagged [E] (ledger rule 13: [E] for the distinction, [H] for this
  group); or "repetition solves it" appears untagged (rule 15); or the matrix has a villain in it
  (a payoff row that only makes sense if one member is assumed malicious, `03` ch 5).
- **0.** No s2.

#### Criterion 2. The equilibrium-selection plan respects risk dominance

*Where to look:* s7, with s5 (threshold, k) and s6 (weak-link unit size). Laws 2 and 3; ledger rules
2 and 13; t04.

- **4.** The regime is diagnosed (mixing, learning rule, talk, group size, history) and tagged [H];
  the plan states that absent intervention the group drifts to the safe outcome and names that
  outcome; the correlation devices are listed in cost order with the lone-cooperator cost first and
  a before/after statement of what the lone cooperator loses; the pacing rule is explicit (grow
  from a core with visible history, keep units small and stable while the norm is young, no random
  rotation); the switch signal toward risk dominance is a group-specific observation; where a
  weak-link process exists, the unit whose minimum matters is capped at a handful or the
  aggregation rule is changed; "sweeten the joint payoff" is either absent or explicitly ranked
  last with the reason.
- **3.** The devices and pacing are present, but the lone-cooperator cost is not stated before and
  after, or the regime is asserted without the learning rule, or the switch signal is generic.
- **2.** "We will build trust and momentum" without a device; or raising the reward for joint
  success is the lead lever; or the plan rotates people across units to spread the practice.
- **1.** "The efficient equilibrium is the rational one" or its equivalent ("once people see it
  pays, they will switch") untagged (rule 2); or failure to adopt is attributed to insufficient
  reward or to irrationality (rule 16).
- **0.** No s7.

#### Criterion 3. Expectations are measured, not assumed

*Where to look:* s8 (instrument, sample, date, results or planned results), s1 (reference network),
s14 (the re-measure). Law 4; ledger rules 10, 11; t13; loop station 2.

- **4.** The four elicitation questions are written out for at least one specific behaviour (not a
  value), with the sample drawn from the reference network named in s1, a date, and either results
  or the planned date; private attitudes are collected alongside so the pluralistic-ignorance gap
  can be computed; the norm-based reading is design-sensitivity-caveated (Bicchieri and Xiao 2009
  is cited for direction, ledger row); the plan re-measures after the intervention and s14 says
  what each of the three outcomes (behaviour and expectations moved; behaviour only; expectations
  only) triggers; every norm the plan relies on is assumed conditional until it survives a
  collapse of expectations (rule 10).
- **3.** The instrument exists and is dated, but the sample is the org chart rather than the
  reference network, or the re-measure is missing, or attitudes are collected without the two
  expectation questions.
- **2.** "We surveyed buy-in" or "people agree the norm matters" stands in for the measurement; or
  the measurement is planned with no date and no instrument.
- **1.** Expectations are asserted from the learner's own belief ("everyone knows we expect tests")
  and tagged [E]; or an expectations campaign is run across clusters that cannot see each other
  (t13 never-clause).
- **0.** No measurement anywhere.

#### Criterion 4. Norms carry both expectation types

*Where to look:* s8 register, each row; s11 two-part norm test. Ledger rules 11, 24; t10; law 5.

- **4.** Every rule the brief relies on is classified with the four-way taxonomy and the
  classification is argued (what most do; what most think others should do; sanction present or
  not); for every social norm, the empirical and the normative expectation are stated as two
  separate sentences and the plan says how each is created or repaired; descriptive and injunctive
  signals are paired in every message the plan sends, and no message advertises low adoption; at
  least one rule is correctly identified as a convention (self-enforcing, needs no maintenance) or
  a descriptive regularity that is not a norm; conventions are used wherever possible for
  coordination goods (`08` implications).
- **3.** Classification and both expectations are present for the main norm but one secondary rule
  is called a "norm" without classification, or a message pairs the signals inconsistently.
- **2.** "Social norm" is used for "what most people do" (rule 11), or normative expectations are
  named but no mechanism creates them beyond a values statement (`08` [V]).
- **1.** A message of the form "too many people still skip X" appears in the plan (law 5 violation,
  caps at 1); or a values statement is presented as the norm intervention and tagged [E].
- **0.** No register.

#### Criterion 5. Sanctions are cheap and graduated; monitoring is a by-product

*Where to look:* s9 (ladder, monitoring, forum), s2 (only bites if the shape is a dilemma or has a
dilemma margin), Deliverable 3 part 5. Law 9; ledger rules 4, 30; t02; t12.

- **4.** A written ladder whose first step is small, quick, public and cheap for the sanctioner
  (a comment, an unapproved review, a name on a rota), with escalation for repeat or flagrant
  cases and a stated top step; monitoring named as a by-product of the work itself (visible commits,
  public logs, turn-taking that exposes cheaters) with no separate policing role that nobody
  wants; a dispute forum that is fast, local and cheap; the sanctioner's cost stated and the
  ladder designed so that the first step is cheaper than looking away; where a stag hunt was
  diagnosed, the section says the ladder is a backstop not the engine; where a metric is used, the
  unmeasured effort it displaces is named and watched (t12); the antisocial-punishment risk is named
  with its check.
- **3.** The ladder exists and monitoring is by-product, but the sanctioner's cost or the forum is
  missing, or the top step is undefined.
- **2.** "Graduated sanctions" appears without the steps, or monitoring is a dashboard someone must
  maintain, or accountability is a leaderboard with no crowding-out watch.
- **1.** The first response to a violation is severe (a ban, a lockout, removal) untagged; or the
  ladder is justified by "people are evolved to punish cheats" tagged [E] (rule 4); or a raise or
  bonus is the engine of contribution (rule 28).
- **0.** No sanction design at all in a brief whose s2 found a dilemma.

#### Criterion 6. The brief names who has standing to change the rules

### Criterion 10. The design survives the Gintis-style critique of its own rationality assumptions

*Where to look:* s11 (signal, two-part norm test, BPC statement, layers), Part A and Part B of the

makes of `11`: a design that says "members will reach the cooperative equilibrium because it is

- **3.** s11 and the defense are sound, but one coordination point has no named signal, or the BPC
  statement omits beliefs (constraints and preferences only), or one Part B answer has four moves,
  or one value attack is answered with evidence rather than a steelman.
- **2.** s11 lists "clear communication" as the signal, or the defense answers are correct
  generalities about other groups, or Part A finds nothing.
- **1.** "Once people see it is rational they will cooperate" untagged; or "people are irrational"

- **0.** No s11 and no defense.

## Worked exemplar: what a 4 looks like

The excerpts below are from a hypothetical learner's v6 for a forty-person open-source project with
a twelve-person reviewer guild; the target behaviour is "every qualifying pull request carries a test
line". Every number in the excerpts is the learner's own prediction or count, not a ledger figure,
and is labelled so. The annotations say why each earns a 4 and what would drop it to 3.

### Criterion 1 (s2), excerpt

> **The game, for a guild member deciding whether to write the test line on a qualifying PR.**
> Players: me, and the other eleven reviewers as "the rest". Options: write (W) or skip (S). Payoffs,
> ordinal, my own estimate from the last quarter's review log [H]: if the rest write and I write, I
> get a suite I can rely on and pay about an hour per PR (call it 3); if the rest write and I skip, I
> get the suite and save the hour (4); if the rest skip and I write, I pay the hour for a suite that
> is broken anyway (0); if the rest skip and I skip, I save the hour and have no suite (1). Order:
> simultaneous within a sprint, repeated across sprints among the same twelve. Information: my
> choice is visible in the review log within a day.
>
> **Defection question.** Does a member gain by skipping when everyone else writes? Yes: 4 over 3.
> So at the sprint margin this is a dilemma, not a stag hunt [H: the payoffs are my estimate; the
> distinction itself is exact, `04` ch 4, ledger rule 13]. It is a repeated dilemma with fast
> detection among a fixed twelve, so a cooperative equilibrium exists at layer 1 if detection is
> used [E for the existence result, `04` ch 10; H that the guild will coordinate on it, rule 15].
>
> **Fix set licensed:** payoffs (lower the hour), repetition (already present), detection and
> sanction (a review line, an unapproved review). **Fix set forbidden as the engine:** assurance
> devices alone (pledges, talk), because talk decays where defection dominates [E in the lab, H
> here; rule 12]. They stay as the layer-4 launch, not the layer-1 fix.
>
> **Not a game:** "reviewers are too slow to also write tests" was on v0. The review log shows the
> guild's capacity exceeds the qualifying-PR rate by my count, so this is not an arithmetic
> constraint [H, re-check monthly, s12-03]. Dropped from the fix list.

*Why it earns a 4.* The matrix is drawn with players, options, ordinal payoffs, order and
information; the defection question is answered with the group's own numbers and the answer drives
the shape; the shape is tagged [H] while the distinction is tagged [E], which is ledger rule 13
exactly; the licensed and forbidden fix sets are both stated, and the forbidden set is placed at
the right layer instead of being thrown away; a v0 belief is tested against the arithmetic and
dropped; there is no villain in the matrix (the payoff for skipping is the same for everyone).

*What would drop it to 3.* Leaving out the "not a game" check, or writing "this is a repeated PD so
tit-for-tat will hold it" (rule 15 forbids tit-for-tat as a rule; the fix set would then need a
"nice, retaliatory, forgiving, clear" statement), or stating the payoffs without the review-log
source. Writing "this is a PD" with no defection question would drop it to 2.

### Criterion 3 (s8), excerpt

> **Elicitation, run 14 September, guild only (the reference network from s1: the twelve who review
> each other's PRs; the docs team and drive-by contributors do not watch the guild's log and are
> excluded from this sample [H that this is the right boundary; s12-05]).** Behaviour asked about:
> "adding a test line to a qualifying PR", not "caring about quality". Four questions, answered
> privately, plus a fifth on private attitude.
>
> - What do you do on a qualifying PR? 5 of 12 said they usually add the line.
> - What do most guild members do? 9 of 12 believed most skip.
> - What do most guild members think you should do? 3 of 12 believed most think you should add it;
>   7 said "nobody minds either way".
> - What happens if you skip? 11 of 12: nothing.
> - Privately, should qualifying PRs carry a test line? 10 of 12 said yes.
>
> **Reading [H, design-sensitive; Bicchieri and Xiao 2009 for the direction that empirical
> expectations of skipping suppress writing even with pro-writing messages].** The empirical
> expectation is "most skip"; the normative expectation is absent; private attitude is strongly
> pro-writing. This is a descriptive norm of skipping held up by pluralistic ignorance, not a social
> norm of skipping, and no social norm of writing exists yet. The gap (10 of 12 privately for, 3 of
> 12 believing others are for) is the thing to publish, from a guild member not from me [H, s12-06].
>
> **Re-measure:** same five questions, same twelve, on 26 October and 7 December (s14). If behaviour
> rises and both expectations move, extend to the docs team through the two parallel ties in s10.
> If behaviour rises and expectations do not, the change rides on the review-line sanction and will
> decay if it lapses: add the guild forum's first revision to the agenda. If expectations move and
> behaviour does not, s2 was wrong about the hour's cost: lower it before doing anything else.

*Why it earns a 4.* The instrument asks about a specific behaviour, not a value; the sample is the
reference network, with the reason the others are excluded stated as an [H]; empirical and normative
expectations are separated and each is read; private attitude is collected so the gap is computable;
the taxonomy is applied and the result is "descriptive norm plus pluralistic ignorance", which
licenses a different lever than "social norm of skipping" would; the re-measure has dates and all
three outcomes have a named branch that returns to a loop station. The counts are the learner's
own and tagged as such; the only citation is for direction.

*What would drop it to 3.* Sampling the whole project instead of the guild, or omitting the private
attitude question (then the gap is a guess), or leaving the re-measure undated. Replacing the four
questions with "how much do you agree that tests matter?" would drop it to 2.

### Criterion 7 (s10), excerpt

> **Copying kind.** Adding a test line is coordination on a graph, not an informational cascade:
> the payoff to writing rises with the fraction of my neighbours who write (a suite is only useful
> if most of it exists), so the fix is lower q or seed inside clusters, not "collect private signals
> first" [E for the classification scheme, ledger rule 19; H that this is the right kind here].
> **q in words:** the cost of writing is the hour (b); the gain when neighbours write is a suite I
> can rely on (a). Right now a is small because the suite is patchy, so q is high; the test template
> and the fixture library in s7 lower b and raise a, so q falls [H, measure by the share of guild
> members who switch after two neighbours do, s12-08].
> **Clusters.** The guild has two clusters by my sketch of who reviews whom: the platform four
> (dense, review each other daily) and the eight app reviewers (looser). The platform four already
> write tests. The eight are the resisting cluster and they block only while none of them has
> switched [E, `10` ch 19 cluster theorem]. Seed: two of the eight, chosen because each is reviewed
> by three of the other six (position, not seniority), and because in the elicitation both were in
> the five who already write [H, s12-09]. **Bridges.** The docs team's only tie to the guild is one
> liaison; that carries awareness and not adoption, so before extending, two docs members will
> pair-review with guild members for a month to make a wide bridge [E for simple vs complex
> contagion, rule 34; H here]. **Learning rule.** s3 says the project copies the fastest shippers
> (success bias), which is why skipping spread; clusters help only if writing looks like success,
> so the weekly demo will show the platform four's cycle time next to their test coverage [H,
> s12-10; if the guild copies the majority instead, seeding will entrench skipping and the
> switch signal is the share of writers in the eight falling after seeding].

*Why it earns a 4.* The copying kind is named and matched to its fix; q is reasoned from the costs
rather than quoted; the seed is inside the resisting cluster with the theorem's condition stated;
the bridge to the next group is widened rather than left to the liaison, with the simple-versus-
complex distinction doing the work; the learning-rule condition is stated and tied to s3, and the
plan says what happens if the condition fails. Nothing about influencers or six degrees.

*What would drop it to 3.* Seeding inside the cluster without saying which copying bias the group
runs on, or naming the seeds by seniority ("the two most respected reviewers") without the position
argument, or leaving q unestimated. Sending the docs team an announcement through the liaison and
expecting adoption would drop it to 2.
